Why pace, not HR, for quality? Right now your pace barely changes across a huge HR range — you run about the same speed at 130 bpm as at 180 bpm, because the aerobic base is still new. So an HR target on intervals would just be your easy pace with a higher number — no actual faster running. Pace bands force the genuine effort. As the base builds, HR and pace will start to line up, and by Block 2 we can bring HR back in for the faster work.

Strides and hills are by feel — no pace or HR target. On a 12–20 second effort neither number has time to mean anything. Run them relaxed and fast (strides) or powerful and controlled (hills); the goal is mechanics and cadence, not a figure on the watch.

One habit to fix: your easy runs have been landing in Zone 3–4 — easy run too hard. On easy days, let HR cap you at 156 even if the pace feels silly-slow. That discipline is what makes the quality days — and the PBs — possible.

The Bigger Picture

Block 1 of 3. Base & Build (here, ending in a HM TT) → a Speed block (VO2 / faster work) → a Half-Marathon-specific block into Hackney, 16 May 2027. Paces and targets reset at each boundary off real data — nothing downstream is locked. Finish this block and you've already run a half; everything after makes it faster.

Everyday Fuelling — Principles, Not Rules

  • Eat enough to fuel the training. Volume and intensity both climb across this block — this is not the time to eat less. Under-fuelling is the fastest route to injury, illness and stalled progress.
  • Carbs are the running fuel. Carb-based meal or snack in the hours before quality and long runs, and again after. Bread, rice, pasta, oats, fruit, potatoes — normal food.
  • Protein for recovery. Include a protein source with meals, especially after runs and strength.
  • Don't run long or hard on empty. Eat beforehand rather than heading out fasted.

Energy availability matters. For female runners especially, eating too little for the training load disrupts hormones, bone health and recovery (RED-S). Flat runs, poor sleep or cycle changes are a signal to eat more, not train harder.

Why a Time Trial, Not Just a Long Run

Running the 21.1km as a TT off a taper gives Bella a real goal to point at, a genuine first-half achievement, and a proper performance marker to set the next blocks' paces from — far more motivating and more useful than grinding out 21.1km as another easy Saturday.

Daily Flags

  • Resting HR 5+ bpm above normal, two mornings: make that day easy/short, or rest.
  • Sharp pain, or pain worsening during a run: stop. Sharp ≠ sore. Two days off; if still there, see a physio.
  • Shin / knee / Achilles ache beyond 48h: cut volume, keep strength, don't push through.
  • Under 5h sleep or run-down: downgrade quality to easy, or skip. Costs nothing here.
  • Low motivation + aches 3 days straight: take an unplanned easy week. Not a setback.

Session Signals

  • Easy pace dropping at the same HR: it's working. Change nothing.
  • Can't hit threshold pace: slow 5–8s/km and call it sub-threshold — still effective.
  • Long run falls apart in the second half twice running: hold that distance an extra week.
  • Cadence stuck below ~155: keep the strides & hills; think 'quick, light feet' on easy runs too.

Missed Sessions

Don't stack them — a missed run is gone; the next session is whatever was already planned. She self-schedules and moves sessions in the app, so slide a run to another day in the same week (keep a rest day between hard days). Protect the long run and the two parkruns — drop an easy run before those.

Repeating a Week Is Not Failure

Around a quarter of new runners repeat a week somewhere to progress pain-free. If a distance or session isn't ready to grow, hold it. That's progressive overload done right.
